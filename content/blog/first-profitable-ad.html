Most first ads lose money for one reason: nobody worked out what "profitable" meant before spending. Whether you're a creator selling a product or a founder running your first campaign, the order of the steps matters more than the budget.

This is the walkthrough we'd give a friend who is about to run their first paid ad.

Step 1: Know your break-even number

Before you open an ad account, work out how much each sale actually leaves you after costs.

  • Selling price: ₹2,000
  • Cost to make, pack and ship: ₹1,200
  • Left over per sale: ₹800, which is a 40% margin

Your break-even return on ad spend (ROAS) is the selling price divided by what's left over: ₹2,000 ÷ ₹800 = 2.5. Until your ads return more than ₹2.50 in sales for every ₹1 spent, you are losing money on each sale. Use your own numbers. Everything else in the campaign is judged against this one.

Step 2: Make one clear offer

An ad sells one thing to one person. Pick the product, bundle or call you most want to sell, and make the offer plain: what it is, who it's for and what they get. A confusing offer cannot be rescued by good targeting.

Step 3: Start with one audience

Choose the group most likely to want this: people who already follow you, visit your site or fit a clear description of your best customers. Keep it simple at first. Too many audiences split your budget so thinly that you learn nothing from any of them.

Step 4: Make the creative do the work

For most people, the ad itself is the biggest lever.

  • Hook early. The first couple of seconds, or the first line, must give the right person a reason to stop.
  • One message per ad. One problem, one promise, one next step.
  • Show the real thing. A person using the product, or you speaking to the camera, usually beats a polished graphic. Creators already have this advantage.
  • Tell people what to do. "Shop now", "Book a call" or "Get the guide", and make sure the link goes to the right page.

Step 5: Send people to a page that finishes the job

Check the page on a phone. It should load quickly, repeat the offer from the ad, and make the next step obvious. A good ad sending people to a slow or confusing page is the most common leak.

Step 6: Set up tracking before you spend

Make sure the platform can see purchases or enquiries, not just clicks. If you can't tell which sales came from which ad, you can't judge profit. Test it with a real order or a test enquiry first.

Step 7: Test small, and decide what you're testing

Spend an amount you can afford to lose, and decide in advance what you want to learn: which hook works, which audience responds, which offer converts. Change one thing at a time. Give each test enough days to collect results before judging it, and don't switch off an ad after a few hours.

Step 8: Read the results against your break-even

Look at the return on ad spend and compare it with your break-even number from Step 1.

  • Above break-even: you've found something. Increase the budget gradually and keep watching.
  • Close to break-even: improve the creative, the offer or the page and test again.
  • Well below: pause and rethink the offer or the audience before spending more.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Spending before knowing the margin.
  • Boosting posts without a clear goal or tracking.
  • Changing everything at once, so you can't tell what worked.
  • Judging results too early, or scaling too fast after one good day.
